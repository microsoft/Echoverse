Compounding cadence

Annually 1× per year▾
Interest is credited once a year. The simplest schedule, often used for long-horizon retirement projections.
Semi-Annually 2× per year▾
Interest compounds twice a year (every 6 months). Common for bond coupons.
Quarterly 4× per year▾
Interest compounds every 3 months. Frequently used by money-market funds and HSAs.
Monthly 12× per year▾
Interest compounds every month — the typical schedule for high-yield savings and CD accounts.
Bi-Weekly 26× per year▾
Interest compounds every two weeks. Useful when modeling payroll-aligned contributions.
Daily 365× per year▾
Interest compounds every day. Maximizes APY for a given nominal rate; common at online banks.
Continuously ∞ per year▾
The theoretical upper bound: FV = P · e^(rt). Used in academic finance and Black-Scholes models.
